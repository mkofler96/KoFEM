# Changelog

User-visible changes to KoFEM, newest first. Each section is written once, by the
release PR that introduces the version. Feature PRs do not edit this file. See
[Releases in AGENTS.md](AGENTS.md#releases).

Changes to numerical results, solver defaults or saved-model compatibility are
always listed, under **Changed** or **Breaking**, with what moved and why.

Versions 0.1.0 to 0.3.0 were tagged after the fact, on the commits that completed
each theme. kofem.org was deployed continuously from `main` throughout, so their
dates are when the work landed, not when anything was shipped.

## [0.5.0] - 2026-11-01

A guided first simulation, public validation examples, and corrections to loads
and shell/solid joints.

### Added

- **Try a sample simulation** opens a wall bracket as CAD geometry and guides you
  through meshing, supports, loading and solving.
- Example and validation pages at `/examples/`, with models you can open in
  KoFEM, reference calculations, convergence studies and limitations. Older
  `/validation/` links redirect to the corresponding example pages.
- **Feedback** offers problem reports and suggestions, with optional diagnostics
  that omit geometry, files and results.
- The engine starts loading when the app opens. The status bar shows progress,
  failures and **Retry**.
- Opt-in workflow analytics, with **Cookie settings** in the app when analytics
  are enabled.

### Changed

- Saved analyses retain their linear or quadratic element order. Older analyses
  without an order still open as linear.
- The cantilever example opens ready to solve and compares its result with a
  hand calculation.
- Validation cases use the same loads as the app. Published results are now
  0.47358 mm axial extension, 2.7750 plate-with-hole stress concentration, and
  24.042 dimensionless Cook's membrane deflection. The benchmark models and
  loads were corrected; acceptance tolerances are unchanged.

### Fixed

- Clicking inside a selected curved region deselects it.
- The status bar counts face and shell edge loads.
- Solid surface loads warn when some selected faces cannot be matched to the
  mesh. Malformed force and traction vectors are rejected with clear errors.

### Breaking

- **Shell/solid joints change when re-meshed.** Shell meshing retains three wall
  thicknesses of solid material at planar joints and connects the shell through
  its physical footprint. Re-mesh and re-solve existing shell models to obtain
  these transitions. In tested tension and bending cases, comparisons with an
  all-solid model on the same mesh stayed within 0.36% for peak junction stress
  and 0.34% for the block stress field.
- **Coupled and shell surface-load results can change.** Total forces distribute
  by area or edge length, traction acts per unit area, and pressure is applied.
  One graded-face test changes its moment from 257.142857 to 240 N·mm; pressure
  previously omitted contributes the intended −120 N. Re-solve affected
  analyses, including coupled topology optimizations. Saved results do not
  update automatically.
- **Conflicting displacement constraints stop the solve.** A component cannot
  be fixed at zero and prescribed to a nonzero displacement, or prescribed to
  two different values. Remove the conflicting group or make its values agree.
  Identical duplicates remain valid.

### Known limitations

- Only one body per model can be a Shell.
- The planar shell/solid transition checks do not establish junction-stress
  accuracy for every model. Generic distributing couplings retain their
  existing junction-stress limitations.
- Coarse linear tetrahedra can make thin-walled solid models too stiff. In a
  reported 3 mm bracket, displacement was 0.0275 mm with linear elements versus
  0.0560 mm with quadratic elements. Use quadratic elements or an appropriate
  Shell idealisation and check convergence.
- A reported meshing crash on complex STEP/IGES models remains unresolved and
  is deferred to 0.6.0.

## [0.4.0] - 2026-10-24

A model tree, and geometry editing in the app.

### Added

- A model tree on the left with a properties panel on the right. It replaces the
  Geometry / Constraints / Solve / Optimize / Results step tabs. Each group
  (Geometry, Materials, Mesh, Boundary conditions, Studies, Results) lists its
  objects and ends in **+ Add**, and picking an entry opens only that object's
  editor. On small screens the tree and the panel share one drawer.
- A split tool that cuts faces or bodies with a plane. Splitting a face gives you
  a separate pad for a support or a load. Splitting a body cuts it into two
  bonded bodies. You find it in a body's panel, and a split can be undone. The
  geometry view now draws CAD face outlines, so you can see a split.
- Boolean bodies: union, subtract (optionally keeping the tool bodies, bonded in
  the pocket) and intersect, from Geometry → **+ Add**. A Boolean that would
  change nothing is refused with a message naming the bodies.
- Per-body visibility, and a **Suppressed** option that leaves a body out of the
  next mesh without deleting it.
- Studies and result plots. Add a static study or a topology optimization, and
  pick the static case an optimization runs on when you add it. Add plots of any
  static result field or of the optimization density. A finished run opens a
  plot of its own result.
- An animated homepage that walks through a bracket from geometry to mesh,
  loads, results and topology optimization.

### Changed

- Supports and loads made on a whole CAD face survive a re-mesh. Edge and node
  picks and couplings are still cleared, and a notice names the ones that were.
- A support or load you just added opens as saved, without asking for a second
  Save.
- "Constraints" is now called "Boundary conditions".
- The MBB beam example is now built the way you would build it in the app: a
  plain block whose bearing and load pads are cut with the split tool.

### Fixed

- After a body was split, one half could render open and see-through when the
  other half was hidden or dimmed.

### Breaking

- **Shells are no longer detected automatically.** Every body imports as Solid.
  To mesh a thin-walled body as shells, set its **Element type** to Shell. Until
  you do, re-importing a thin-walled part that used to be shelled automatically
  meshes and solves it as solid, and its results differ.
- **A body marked Shell is now shelled as a whole.** Before, a thick region such
  as a mounting block stayed solid. Now a Shell body whose thin walls do not cover
  all of it is refused, and the message says how much of it would be lost. Split
  the thick part off into its own Solid body first.
- Saved analyses still open unchanged, with their saved mesh and results.

### Known limitations

- Only one body per model can be a Shell. Marking a second one is refused with
  an error.
- A distributing coupling between a shell and a solid transmits the right force
  and gives the right deflection, but the stress it leaves in the solid at the
  joint is about 7× too low and does not improve with a finer mesh. Do not rely
  on junction stress from a distributing coupling. This was already the case in
  0.3.0.

## [0.3.0] - 2026-09-28

Topology optimization.

### Added

- Topology optimization with SIMP and the MMA optimizer, from a new **Optimize**
  step:
  - Minimum compliance, minimum volume under a compliance limit, and
    stress-constrained optimization.
  - A density/sensitivity filter that keeps results mesh-independent.
  - A reaction–diffusion level-set optimizer as an alternative to SIMP.
- Works on solid, shell and coupled shell/solid models.
- The density field streams live while the optimizer runs. Afterwards you get a
  threshold slider, a convergence plot, and STL export of the optimized shape.
- The MBB beam as a topology-optimization example in the gallery.
- Validation benchmarks: the MBB beam, a 3D cantilever, and a mesh-independence
  check.

### Changed

- The CG solver tolerance is configurable for optimization runs. Plain static
  solves keep their tolerance.

### Removed

- The crane hook example is off the gallery until its result is validated.

## [0.2.0] - 2026-09-08

Shells and multibody.

### Added

- Kirchhoff (DKT) shell elements, alongside solids.
- Automatic shell detection: at mesh time, thin walls become shells by
  wall-thickness ratio and are stored as a mixed shell/solid model. You can turn
  the switch and its ratio on or off for every model.
- Per-body element type (shell or solid) and per-body materials for multibody
  assemblies. Bodies are joined by bonded ties.
- Explicit RBE3 coupling between shells and solids, explicit tie connections, and
  surface-to-point couplings.
- Prescribed (non-zero) displacements on shell models.
- Edge picking on shell rims.
- Manual colorbar limits for result fields.
- The shell plate-with-hole example in the gallery.

### Changed

- The default mesh size follows the geometry instead of a fixed value, so meshes
  of unchanged models differ from 0.1.0.

### Fixed

- Loads on surfaces that share elements are all applied. Before, one could
  silently replace another.
- Ties that couple nothing, unmatched surface loads, and BC or load indices out
  of range now raise an error instead of being skipped.
- The coupled auto-shell tie and preconditioner now work on fine meshes.

## [0.1.0] - 2026-07-05

The solid pipeline.

### Added

- STEP and IGES import. Loose IGES surfaces are sewn into solids, and imported
  CAD is healed before meshing.
- Linear-elastic solids with first- or second-order tetrahedra, and nodal-averaged
  results.
- Loads:
  - Componentwise forces and moments.
  - Pressure, applied as work-equivalent surface tractions.
  - Per-node load glyphs.
- Constraints: fixed constraints and prescribed (non-zero) displacements.
- A deformation-scale slider, live CG convergence in the log panel, inline
  editing of BC and load values, and a "New analysis" reset.
- Validation cases, including beam torsion and a second-order regression case.

### Changed

- Units are N·mm·MPa throughout.

### Fixed

- These now fail with an error instead of a plausible-looking result:
  - a CG solve that does not converge;
  - a missing or invalid material;
  - a zero or non-finite load;
  - a multi-material model.
