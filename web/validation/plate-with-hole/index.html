Plate with a hole (Kirsch)

A small circular hole in a plate under tension raises the stress at its edge to three times the nominal value. Kirsch's solution (1898) for this stress concentration is the classic test of how well a solver resolves a stress peak, a harder target than a displacement, which is what the finite element method approximates most accurately.

Analytical solution

Take an infinite plate under uniaxial tension σ along x, with a hole of radius a. In polar coordinates (r,θ) about the hole centre, with θ measured from the load direction, Kirsch's solution of the plane elasticity problem gives the hoop stress

σθθ=σ2(1+a2r2)−σ2(1+3a4r4)cos2θ.

On the edge of the hole, r=a, the radial and shear stresses vanish because the surface is free, and

σθθ(a,θ)=σ(1−2cos2θ).

This is largest at θ=±90°, the points A across the load direction, and the stress concentration factor is

Kt=σmaxσ=3.

At θ=0 the edge is in compression, σθθ=−σ, although the plate is pulled. Along the transverse axis the stress decays quickly with the distance from the hole,

σxx(0,y)=σ(1+a22y2+3a42y4),

to 1.22σ at y=2a and 1.04σ at y=4a (figure, right). At the edge it falls steeply, dσxx/dy=−7σ/a.

The solution does not depend on Poisson's ratio, so it holds in plane stress and plane strain alike. A plate of finite half-width b raises Kt slightly; for a/b=0.1, as here, by about 1 % (Peterson's), so Kt=3 is a good reference.

Finite element model

MeshO-grid, 12 rings × 64 around the hole, 1 through t: 768 linear 8-node hexahedra (P1)
Unknowns1,664 nodes, 4,992 degrees of freedom
MaterialLinear elastic, isotropic
SolverKoFEM engine (MFEM), the same WebAssembly build the app runs: engine-bb451aa7afb6

The O-grid has 12 rings of 64 elements, graded so the rings are thinnest at the hole, with one element through the thickness. KoFEM reports one von Mises stress per element, at its centre; the case takes the largest value in the ring around the hole and divides it by σ. At A the surface is free and the only stress is σθθ, so there the von Mises stress equals σθθ and its peak is 3σ.

Discussion

KoFEM's Kt differs from 3 by −4.30 %. Two effects meet here. First, the measured stress is not the stress at the edge but at the centre of the first element, a small distance away, where the stress has already started to fall. Kirsch's solution at that point, r=1.031a and θ=87.2°, gives a von Mises stress of 2.75σ, 8.4 % below the peak. Even exact element stresses could not reach 3 with this measure.

Second, element stresses carry their own discretisation error. Stress comes from derivatives of the displacement field, so it converges more slowly than the displacement does; here it lands above the exact value at the element centre. The published 2.87 is the net of both. That is why this case has a wider ±15 % band. Treat it as a check that the peak is in the right place and of the right size. To resolve a peak stress in a design, refine the mesh at the stress raiser until the peak stops changing.

Reproduce

From a clone (Node.js 20+, bash, curl), this solves the case with the same engine and prints its line:

git clone https://github.com/mkofler96/KoFEM.git && cd KoFEM
bash scripts/fetch-wasm-engine.sh
node examples/validation/run.mjs --case plate-with-hole
