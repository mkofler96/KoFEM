Analytical solution

Euler–Bernoulli beam theory assumes that cross-sections stay plane and perpendicular to the deformed axis, which neglects shear deformation. Take w(x) as the deflection, positive upwards, with the load P acting downwards at the tip. Equilibrium of the part beyond a section x gives the bending moment

M(x)=−P(L−x),

and the curvature is proportional to it:

EId2wdx2=M(x),I=bh312.

Integrating twice with the clamp conditions w(0)=0 and dwdx(0)=0 gives the deflection curve

w(x)=−Px26EI(3L−x),

and at the tip

δ=w(L)=−PL33EI.

For this beam I=8.3333×10−6 m4 and δ=−1.9048×10−3 m.

Timoshenko beam theory adds the shear deformation that Euler–Bernoulli leaves out. For a cantilever with a tip load it adds a second term,

δs=−PLκGA,κ=56,G=E2(1+ν),

which is 0.78 % of the bending term for this beam (span ten times the depth). A converged 3-D solid model should therefore deflect slightly more than PL3/(3EI).

Finite element model

Mesh10 × 2 × 2 grid: 40 quadratic 27-node hexahedra (P2)
Unknowns525 nodes, 1,575 degrees of freedom
MaterialLinear elastic, isotropic
SolverKoFEM engine (MFEM), the same WebAssembly build the app runs: engine-bb451aa7afb6

A quadratic hexahedron has 27 nodes: at its corners, edge midpoints, face centres and centre. The displacement varies quadratically along each edge, so an element can curve. Clamping the x=0 face fixes all of its nodes, including the mid-edge and mid-face ones. The 40 elements have 525 nodes and 1,575 unknowns, about half the 3,075 of the linear case. The tip load is split equally over the element vertices of the end face; its mid-side nodes carry no load.

Discussion

On this coarse mesh the deflection differs from the Euler–Bernoulli value by +1.88 % and is close to the Timoshenko value that includes shear deformation, −1.9196×10−3 m. A quadratic element can represent a linearly varying bending strain exactly: its edges curve, so it bends without parasitic shear and does not lock.

Linear elements on meshes this coarse lock badly. In the convergence study below, the error is −35.22 % with 10 × 1 × 1 linear elements and −12.42 % with 20 × 2 × 2; the quadratic series is within a few percent on every mesh. For bending-dominated parts, quadratic elements give more accuracy per unknown.
