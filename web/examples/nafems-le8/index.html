NAFEMS LE8: axisymmetric shell under pressure

A closed shell of revolution: a cylinder, a tight toroidal knuckle and a spherical end dished inward, under internal pressure. Where the knuckle meets the end, at D, the shell bends hard. NAFEMS gives the hoop stress on the outer surface there, σθθ=94.5 MPa.

Rend250 mm
t10 mm
E210 GPa
ν0.3
p1 MPa
SupportsEnd A held axially; symmetry on the cuts θ = 0 and θ = 90°, which also holds the pole.
LoadInternal pressure 1 MPa: −1 MPa on the outer faces of the three walls.
Measuredσyy on the outer (top) surface at D on the cut θ = 0, read with a probe: the hoop stress.

Analytical solution

A shell of revolution under pressure carries it as a membrane where its curvature changes slowly, with meridional and hoop forces set by the two radii of curvature r1 and r2:

Nφr1+Nθr2=p.

At a knuckle the meridian's radius jumps from the torus to the sphere, the membrane forces cannot follow, and the shell bends: the stress at D is mostly that bending.

Finite element model

MeshNetgen, 7.5 mm maximum element size: 10,950 linear 3-node shell triangles (CST membrane, DKT bending)
Unknowns5,611 nodes, 33,666 degrees of freedom
MaterialLinear elastic, isotropic
SolverThe app's shell solver (conjugate gradients, KoFEM's Rust module, build fem-9e0ba78fc956); geometry and mesh by the KoFEM engine (OCCT, Netgen), the WebAssembly build the app runs: engine-44314641a642

NAFEMS gives the dimensions in a figure: the cylinder radius, the height of the pole, the knuckle and end radii and an angle of about 36°. With the knuckle tangent to the end and the end's centre on the axis, the knuckle arc spans 36.87°, which fixes every point. A 90° sector, its cut meridians held by symmetry, is imported as a 10 mm solid and marked Shell: the app collapses the cylinder, the torus and the sphere to one welded mid-surface, all kept on their outer faces. The pressure acts on the outer faces, pulling outward, and the probe reads σyy, the hoop stress on the cut y = 0, on the top surface at D.

10,950 flat triangles.

Discussion

KoFEM reads −7.70 % from the NAFEMS target, the widest difference of the NAFEMS pages, and the band is wide to match. Thin shell theory does not reach the target either: an axisymmetric Kirchhoff shell solution of this geometry converges about 7 % below it, and Abaqus' own axisymmetric shell elements read about 4 % below. KoFEM's curved shells agree with that thin-shell solution to within 1.5 %, and the convergence study shows them settled. The difference from the target most likely lies in the geometry, which NAFEMS gives only as a figure.

Reproduce

In the app: open the model, with its supports, loads and settings.

Or build it yourself, as this page's number was built:

  1. Import nafems-le8.step.
  2. Set the body to Shell. Meshing measures the 10 mm wall and replaces it by its mid-surface; the faces through the wall become the shell's edges, which you pick as Edges.
  3. Mesh with a 7.5 mm maximum element size, linear elements.
  4. Material Steel: E = 210000 MPa, ν = 0.3.
  5. Support “Axial end A”: Uz = 0 on the edge through (176.777, 176.777, 0).
  6. Support “Symmetry θ = 0”: Uy, Rx, Rz = 0 on the edge through (250, 0, 250).
  7. Support “Symmetry θ = 90°”: Ux, Ry, Rz = 0 on the edge through (1.53081e-14, 250, 250).
  8. Load “Internal pressure”: pressure −1 MPa on the edges through (180.312, 180.312, 250), (156.447, 156.447, 558.457), (56.5685, 56.5685, 518.429).
  9. Run the static study. The Results panel compares hoop stress at d with NAFEMS LE8 target.
