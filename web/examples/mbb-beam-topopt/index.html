MBB beam, topology optimization

Topology optimization decides where material should go. The MBB beam is its standard test: a simply supported beam with a load at mid-span, and half of the block's volume to spend. The stiffest layout is a truss, with a compression chord on top, a tension tie along the bottom and diagonals between them. The model opens in the Optimize step, ready to run.

The problem

H50 mm

E210 GPa
ν0.3
F1 kN
SupportsBottom-left pad fixed (Ux, Uy, Uz); bottom-right pad Uy only (a roller).
LoadF in −y as a force on the pad at the top of mid-span.
MeasuredCompliance F·u, the work done by the load, at half the volume.

The optimization problem

Each element e gets a density ρe between 0 (void) and 1 (solid). SIMP (solid isotropic material with penalisation) scales the element's stiffness with a power of its density,

Ee=ρepE,p=3,

so intermediate densities give little stiffness for their volume and the optimum is pushed towards 0 or 1. The problem is to minimise the compliance, the work done by the load, at a fixed volume:

minρc(ρ)=FTu(ρ)subject toV(ρ)V0=0.5.

Lower compliance means a stiffer structure. The sensitivities are filtered over a radius rmin=10 mm, which prevents checkerboard patterns and makes the layout independent of the mesh. The densities are updated by MMA, the method of moving asymptotes, with a move limit of 0.2 per iteration.

There is no closed-form answer to compare with. The check is that the run converges and that the layout matches the published one.

Finite element model

MeshNetgen, 5 mm maximum element size: 7,887 linear 4-node tetrahedra (P1)
Unknowns2,225 nodes, 6,675 degrees of freedom
MaterialLinear elastic, isotropic
SolverKoFEM engine (MFEM), the WebAssembly build the app runs: engine-bb451aa7afb6

The block is a STEP file, cut with the app's split tool so that the two supports and the load each sit on their own CAD face. Netgen meshes it at 5 mm. The whole beam is modelled rather than the symmetric half. The optimizer stops when no density changes by more than 0.01 between iterations, or after 100. The figure shows the elements with ρ≥0.5.

Results

Iterations88, converged (largest density change 6.1221 × 10−3)
Compliance192.55 N·mm at the start, 47.499 N·mm at the end
Volume0.500 of the block (target 0.5)
Elements1,806 solid (ρ > 0.9), 1,913 void (ρ < 0.1)

Discussion

The compliance falls by 75 % from the uniform start, most of it in the first iterations, while the volume stays at the target. The layout is the one in the literature: a chord along the top, a tie along the bottom and diagonals between them, as in Sigmund's 99-line and Andreassen's 88-line codes.

The tetrahedral mesh leaves a ragged surface where the threshold cuts through elements. The layout is the result, not the final part: export it, smooth it and check it with a solve of the solid design.

Reproduce

In the app: open the model, with its supports, loads and settings.

From a clone (Node.js 20+, bash, curl), this rebuilds the model and its figure:

git clone https://github.com/mkofler96/KoFEM.git && cd KoFEM
bash scripts/fetch-wasm-engine.sh
cd web && bun install && bun run examples:generate-mbb-topopt
