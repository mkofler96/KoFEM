NAFEMS LE1: elliptic membrane

The first of the NAFEMS linear-elastic benchmarks, the standard tests commercial codes publish their results for. A quarter of an elliptic ring is pulled outward along its outer edge. Where the inner ellipse is sharpest, at D, the tangential stress peaks at σyy=92.7 MPa.

The problem

t100 mm
E210 GPa
ν0.3
p10 MPa
SupportsUx on the face x = 0, Uy on the face y = 0, Uz on the mid-plane z = 0.
Load10 MPa outward pressure on the outer elliptic face.
Measuredσyy at D = (2000, 0, 0) mm, read with a probe.

Analytical solution

In plane stress the faces of the ring carry no load, so σzz=τxz=τyz=0 through the thickness. At D the free inner edge meets the symmetry line y=0: the stress normal to the edge vanishes and only the tangential stress σyy remains. It peaks there because the inner ellipse is sharpest at D, with the radius of curvature

ρD=b2a=(1 m)22 m=0.5 m.

The ring is too short for a closed-form solution: NAFEMS' target is a converged numerical one.

Finite element model

MeshNetgen, 50 mm maximum element size: 14,455 quadratic 10-node tetrahedra (P2)
Unknowns29,511 nodes, 88,533 degrees of freedom
MaterialLinear elastic, isotropic
SolverThe app's default solver (conjugate gradients with algebraic multigrid, KoFEM's Rust module, build fem-fe1376d90a4d); geometry and mesh by the KoFEM engine (OCCT, Netgen), the WebAssembly build the app runs: engine-44314641a642

The ring is 100 mm thick and in plane stress, so it is modelled as a thin solid: the faces through the thickness are free, and the model keeps half of the thickness, with its mid-plane held in z by symmetry. The cut edges are held by rollers, and the outer face carries an outward pressure of 10 MPa (the app's pressure pushes along the inward normal, so it is entered as negative).

Netgen fills it with 14,455 quadratic tetrahedra. Each element's stress is recovered at its corners and averaged over the elements that meet at a node; a probe placed at D by its coordinates reads σyy there.

The corner stresses of the few elements at D scatter around the peak: 1–2 % high on coarse meshes, a few tenths of a percent on the finest.

The read-out is a stress at a point, recovered at element corners; the rate check covers energy-type results and point displacements only.

Discussion

KoFEM reads +1.65 % from the NAFEMS target. NAFEMS' own coarse meshes of linear triangles read as much as half the peak: a constant-stress element cannot follow the steep rise towards D. Quadratic elements follow it, and the convergence study shows the read-out settling onto the target as the mesh is refined.

Reproduce

In the app: open the model, with its supports, loads and settings.

Or build it yourself, as this page's number was built:

  1. Import nafems-le1.step.
  2. Mesh with a 50 mm maximum element size, quadratic elements.
  3. Material Steel: E = 210000 MPa, ν = 0.3.
  4. Support “Symmetry x = 0”: Ux = 0 on the face through (0, 1800, 10).
  5. Support “Symmetry y = 0”: Uy = 0 on the face through (2600, 0, 10).
  6. Support “Mid-plane z = 0”: Uz = 0 on the face through (1000, 1500, 0).
  7. Load “Outward pressure”: pressure −10 MPa on the face through (2298.1, 1944.54, 10).
  8. Run the static study. The Results panel compares tangential stress at d with NAFEMS LE1 target.

References
