Interpretation and convergence

Which conclusions are justified by a result, and when must the FEM discretisation be refined?

A clean contour and a converged linear solve do not establish an accurate physical prediction. Choose the quantity needed for the engineering decision, understand its recovery, and compare it across suitable meshes and modelling assumptions. This page concerns static, small-strain, isotropic linear-elastic solids.

Choose a meaningful quantity

For a clearance, inspect displacement in the relevant direction at the relevant location. A global displacement magnitude can answer a different question. For a ductile yield check, von Mises may be appropriate; brittle or fatigue assessments need other material data and stress measures. See what von Mises measures.

Record whether the value is a point sample, a regional average or a maximum, and whether it is smoothed. KoFEM’s ordinary solid result has vertex translations and centre-sampled element von Mises stress; the app’s summary reports extrema of the averaged nodal field. A single centre sample is not a bound on stress within an element. A displayed maximum is not automatically the stress at the physical point of interest.

Concentration or singularity?

A smooth hole or finite-radius fillet can produce a large but finite stress concentration. Refinement should resolve the geometry and stress gradient until the chosen measure stabilizes. The plate-with-hole case shows how centre sampling alone can miss a finite boundary peak.

An ideal point force in a 3D continuum, a sharp re-entrant corner, or an abrupt change between fixed and free boundary conditions can produce a stress singularity: the ideal elastic stress grows without a finite limit as distance to the singular point tends to zero. Smaller elements sample closer to it, so the reported peak can keep rising while displacement elsewhere settles.

A rising peak can also mean an unresolved finite concentration or poor element quality; mesh dependence alone does not prove a singularity. Inspect where the peak sits and which idealisation causes it. Where the physical part has a radius or a distributed contact load, model those features if they matter to the question.

If the idealisation is intentional, report a quantity with a justified physical meaning, such as stress at a specified distance or an average over a fixed physical region. Keep that distance or region unchanged during refinement and use an assessment method appropriate to the failure mode. Deleting the hottest elements or changing the averaging region with mesh size does not establish a safe design.

Smoothing can lower a singular peak on screen without removing the singularity from the mathematical model. Linear elasticity also cannot predict plastic redistribution in the real part.

Compare a sequence of discretizations

Use several systematically refined meshes near the feature controlling the chosen result. Keep geometry, material, supports and total loading fixed. Resolve thickness and bending where relevant; one global size does not describe local resolution or element quality.

Let qc and qf be the same scalar readout on consecutive coarse and fine meshes, in the same units. For a nonzero fine value, a useful change indicator is:

Δq=|qf−qc||qf|×100%.

For example, 120 MPa followed by 126 MPa gives 4.76% change. Near zero, use an absolute change with stated units. Choose a tolerance appropriate to the decision and its margin; there is no universal acceptable percentage.

A small consecutive change is evidence of stability, not a proven error bound. Check the trend across further refinements, the peak’s location, and the recovery measure. Results need not converge monotonically. Two coarse meshes can agree because both miss the same feature.

Increasing element order changes the approximation. Linear tetrahedra have constant strain per straight-sided element and can be too stiff in bending. Quadratic elements resolve richer fields, but KoFEM still exports one solid stress sample per element and vertex-only translations. Centre-sample convergence does not establish convergence of the unreported maximum within a quadratic element. See elements and strain recovery.

Keep three accuracy questions separate: the algebraic residual measures the solve of one discrete system; mesh/order convergence probes discretization error; comparison with physical evidence tests modelling assumptions. Tightening a solver tolerance cannot repair a singularity or an incorrect load.

Keep the comparison consistent

Record these with every compared result
ItemWhat must agree or be stated
Physical modelGeometry, material law, dimensional assumptions, supports, load distribution and total load. A shell surface value and a solid centre value measure different locations.
QuantitySame displacement component or magnitude, stress invariant, and point/average/maximum definition. Von Mises cannot substitute for a signed normal stress.
LocationThe same physical location or fixed region, body and side of an interface. Element IDs change after remeshing; they do not define a common location.
RecoveryCentre versus integration-point or extrapolated values; scalar versus tensor averaging; weights and material-interface treatment.
UnitsCoherent lengths, loads and moduli, and the same reported units. 1 MPa = 10⁶ Pa; convert both stiffness inputs and stress outputs.
DiscretizationElement family and order, local sizes, quality and refinement sequence. Record each mesh rather than comparing only element counts.
DisplayCommon legend limits, camera and deformation scale for visual comparison. Use numerical readouts to compare values.

The recovery page documents KoFEM’s averaging, including equal weights for hexes and the absence of a material-boundary filter. The unit reference explains coherent systems without implying automatic conversion.

Explain a surprising result

Displacement looks enormous, but the readout is small.
Inspect the values and the automatic deformation scale. An amplified shape does not demonstrate large physical deformation. If actual rotations or strains are large, revisit the linear model.
The new mesh has a higher peak stress.
Locate it. Check unresolved radii, point loading, fixed/free edges, element quality and sampling distance. Compare values away from a suspected singularity as well as the maximum.
The contour is smooth across a material change.
Shared-node scalar averaging can conceal a stress jump. Different materials can have different strain/stress states even with continuous displacement. A smooth band does not establish interface accuracy.
The app maximum differs from another solver or benchmark.
Match locations, units, stress measures and recovery. A nodal average differs from an unsmoothed centre sample, a surface value or an extrapolated integration-point peak.
A support is hot, or the model is unexpectedly stiff.
Check whether constraints suppress real Poisson contraction or intended rotation, whether disconnected parts retain rigid motions, and whether load transfer/ties match the assembly. Review loads and constraints and element order before changing material values.
Compression and tension have the same von Mises value.
The scalar discards sign. A low von Mises value can also coexist with a large hydrostatic stress. Use an appropriate failure criterion; the static selector does not expose tensor components or principal stresses.

Evidence and further reading

KoFEM-specific statements were reviewed on 6 November 2026 at 0db1e95. Solid recovery supplies centre samples; nodal averaging and extrema defines the app’s summarized measure. The preceding source inventory traces the display and shell/coupled distinctions.

The hole benchmark and its measurement code provide a reproducible comparison. A smooth hole is a finite concentration; this case does not verify singular stresses, plasticity, arbitrary material interfaces or every recovery method.

  • Cook, Malkus, Plesha and Witt, Concepts and Applications of Finite Element Analysis, fourth edition (2002), stress recovery, modelling and convergence.
  • Szabó and Babuška, Finite Element Analysis (1991), discretization error and singular solutions.
  • Timoshenko and Goodier, Theory of Elasticity, third edition (1970), the circular-hole stress concentration.
