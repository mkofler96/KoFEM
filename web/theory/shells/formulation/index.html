How does a flat triangular shell represent membrane and bending response?

A shell replaces a thin wall’s volume with a mid-surface and a thickness. Its assumptions describe how material moves through that thickness. KoFEM combines a constant-strain triangle (CST) for in-plane stretching with a discrete Kirchhoff triangle (DKT) for bending.

Choose the physical idealisation

Use a shell when thickness is small relative to the lengths over which deformation changes, and through-thickness normal and transverse-shear deformation do not control the answer. A thin wall meshed as a solid still solves three-dimensional elasticity with translational unknowns; it needs enough volume elements to resolve bending through its thickness. Refining that mesh does not turn it into a shell.

In KoFEM, choose Solid or Shell explicitly for each body in the geometry panel. Solid is the default. Shell asks the meshing pipeline to identify opposite planar wall faces, collapse them to mid-surfaces and assign their measured normal separation as thickness. It can retain solid material at roots and bulk regions, producing a mixed shell–solid model. Inspect the resulting FEM discretisation before solving.

Keep a region solid when thickness effects, local bearing, a joint or three-dimensional stress is the question. A shell’s surface von Mises value cannot resolve those effects. See retained solid transition regions.

Mid-surface motion and local axes

The formulation is static, small-strain and linear elastic, with an isotropic, homogeneous material through each section. It uses a Kirchhoff–Love assumption: a line initially normal to the mid-surface stays straight, normal and unstretched. There is no transverse-shear energy, geometric nonlinearity, plasticity, buckling analysis or laminate law.

Each three-node facet has six unknowns per node: three translations and three small rotations, giving an 18-component element vector. Both vectors are stored in global axes. Locally, e1 runs from node 0 to node 1, e3 is the normalized cross product of the two edges from node 0, and e2=​e3×e1. Node winding therefore fixes the normal. The code rotates translations and rotations into this right-handed basis for element calculations, then transforms stiffness back to global axes.

Let x,y be local surface coordinates and z the signed distance along e3, with −t/2≤​z≤​t/2. Mid-surface translations are u0,v0,w0; local rotations about e1,e2,e3 are θx,θy,θz. The physical section motion is:

u(x,y,z)=u0(x,y)+zθy(x,y),v(x,y,z)=v0(x,y)−zθx(x,y),w(x,y,z)=w0(x,y).

Zero transverse shear gives θx=​∂w0/∂y and θy=​−∂w0/∂x in the smooth theory. DKT imposes the Kirchhoff constraint discretely, using quadratic rotation interpolation expressed in the three corner nodes’ displacement and rotation coefficients. It needs a connected triangle mesh with shared nodal coefficients, rather than a classical smooth plate approximation with continuous first derivatives. Rotations are independent algebraic unknowns, so they are not obtained by simply differentiating a linear triangle’s displacement.

The rotation θz about the facet normal is the drilling rotation. The physical CST/DKT blocks do not supply its stiffness. KoFEM adds a small diagonal stabilization, 10−4 times the mean bending diagonal per facet. This removes a numerical singularity; it is not a physical torsional constitutive law.

Membrane and bending stiffness

Use the shared engineering-shear convention. The membrane strain vector εm contains two normal strains and the in-plane engineering shear. The curvature vector κ has units of inverse length and includes the doubled twisting curvature:

εm=(∂u0/∂x,∂v0/∂y,∂u0/∂y+∂v0/∂x)T,κ=(∂θy/∂x,−∂θx/∂y,∂θy/∂y−∂θx/∂x)T.

The CST membrane strain is constant on a facet. DKT curvature varies across it. With Young’s modulus E and Poisson ratio ν, the plane-stress matrix is:

Cp=E1−ν2[1ν0ν1000(1−ν)/2]

Write εm=​Bmdm and κ=​Bbdb, where the local coefficient vectors contain respectively the six in-plane and nine bending unknowns. On facet area Ae, the two stiffness contributions are:

Km=∫AeBmT(tCp)BmdA,Kb=∫AeBbT(t312Cp)BbdA.

The membrane integral is constant times area. Bending uses three mid-edge points of equal weight Ae/3. The flat, centred, homogeneous section has no membrane–bending material coupling term. Facets in different orientations still interact through shared global unknowns. Curved geometry is approximated by flat facets and needs geometric refinement as well as a displacement check.

Thickness matters strongly: membrane stiffness scales with t, bending stiffness with t3. Doubling thickness multiplies these by 2 and 8. Thickness is a section property, not the triangle edge size. Check both when refining.

Stress resultants and surface stress

Shell force resultants n integrate in-plane stress through thickness; moment resultants m integrate stress times its thickness coordinate. Each is a three-component local vector, ordered xx,yy,xy. For the centred section:

σp(z)=Cp(εm+zκ),n=∫−t/2t/2σp(z)dz=tCpεm,m=∫−t/2t/2zσp(z)dz=t312Cpκ.

With N and mm, stress is MPa, n is N/mm and m is N·mm/mm (numerically N). These resultants are useful theory quantities; KoFEM’s static result does not return them as separate fields. See the shared shell notation.

The implementation evaluates membrane strain and bending curvature at each facet centroid, forms plane-stress von Mises at z=​±t/2, and returns the larger surface value. It returns three translations per node for the displacement field, rather than the six-component internal vector. A surface maximum loses the tension/compression sign and which side governed. It omits transverse normal and transverse shear stress; it is not a three-dimensional junction-stress estimate. Viewport smoothing adds the separate display recovery.

Current inputs and limitations

Shell support in KoFEM
InputCurrent behavior
CAD body selectionOne active body per model may be idealised as Shell. A second is rejected, even if it uses the same material. This guard concerns CAD idealisation, not a limit of one triangle or one wall.
Materials and sectionsExplicit all-CTRIA3 models may have several section properties and per-facet thicknesses, but must reference one material ID. Multiple shell material IDs are rejected. Mixed models also require one shell material; their explicit solid domain supports per-tet materials.
Geometry extractionOpposite planar faces are paired one to one, with planarity/area guards and a wall-separation cap of 15 in the app’s mm coordinates. Convex corners can share mid-surface nodes. Split faces and T-junctions, including I-section flanges, are not generally reconstructed; a converged web-only result does not establish a complete section.
Surface loadsUniform total face force, per-area traction and pressure become facet nodal forces, using one third of the facet contribution at each node. A total edge force is shared by edge length, half to each endpoint. Positive shell pressure acts against the winding normal. Pressure and per-area traction on edges are rejected.
Moments and restraintsPure-shell payloads accept nodal forces/moments and fixed or prescribed components 0–5. App moment groups normally become force couples. Mixed/idealised models use different rotation rules; see the coupling article. An all-shell model with a declared surface-to-point coupling is rejected.

Shell selection expresses modelling intent; it does not prove successful idealisation. Check wall coverage, connectivity, normal direction, thickness and the retained solid. No detected wall produces an explicit error; detecting one wall does not certify that every wall was found. Revisit the idealisation when section geometry, load application or the quantity of interest changes.

Check a uniformly loaded square plate

The existing native production-core test uses a square of side a=​1, thickness t=​0.01, E=​107, ν=​0.3 and uniform transverse load q=​1. One coherent interpretation is m, N, Pa: a=​1 m, t=​0.01 m, E=​107 Pa and q=​1 Pa. These are normalized verification inputs, not the app’s mm/MPa defaults or a steel example. The test applies q toward the positive local normal; this is the opposite sign to positive app shell pressure.

Timoshenko and Woinowsky-Krieger give the small-deflection plate results below, with flexural rigidity D=​Et3/[12(1−ν2)]. wc is centre deflection; Mc is either centre bending moment resultant for the simply supported square:

wcSS=0.00406qa4D,wcclamped=0.00126qa4D,McSS=0.0479qa2.

The mesh has 32 × 32 cells, two DKT/CST triangles per cell, and 6×332=​6534 coefficients before constraints. Clamped edges fix all six components. Simply supported edges fix normal translation and leave tangential bending rotations free; the test fixes in-plane translations and drilling everywhere to isolate bending. Facet area loads are split equally over their three nodes. Native CG uses relative target 10−10.

At the simply supported centre the bending state is equal biaxial, so the surface von Mises reference is 6Mc/t2. Recovery samples the closest facet centroid, not the exact centre. The inherited bands allow discretisation, rounded reference coefficients and that sampling offset; the 8% stress band is a regression threshold, not a general stress accuracy promise. This single mesh verifies these responses, not a convergence rate. The linear calculation also omits membrane stretching caused by large deflection; scale load down when a physical study needs deflection much smaller than thickness.

The numerical record stores every measured value. These runs exercise the production C++ core natively; they do not exercise CAD extraction or the browser/WASM wrappers. The transition comparison addresses a separate load-transfer question.
