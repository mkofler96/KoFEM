How is the linear system solved, and what do tolerance and non-convergence mean?

An iterative solver improves a displacement estimate until an algebraic imbalance is small enough. That establishes how closely it solves the assembled equations. Mesh refinement and comparison with physical evidence answer separate questions. This page covers static, small-strain, isotropic linear-elastic solids; the implementation details refer to KoFEM’s ordinary solid solve. Shell, coupled and optimization solvers have their own settings.

The system after constraints

Assembly collects the element equations. Displacement constraints then move known values to the right-hand side. Write the resulting system as:

Ax=b.

Here A is the constrained stiffness matrix, x contains the displacement coefficients, and b is the modified load vector, including prescribed-displacement contributions. This local b is a discrete vector, not the continuum body-force field. In the N–mm system, their units are N/mm, mm and N. MFEM retains constrained rows with their diagonal entries; the free rows express force balance.

Conjugate gradients and preconditioning

Conjugate gradients (CG) uses sparse matrix–vector products and a few vectors instead of forming a dense inverse. It chooses successive search directions that are orthogonal in the stiffness inner product. In exact arithmetic, CG solves a positive-definite system in at most as many steps as there are unknowns. Rounding and poor conditioning make that bound an impractical iteration budget.

CG assumes a symmetric positive-definite matrix: vTAv>​0 for every nonzero vector v. For elastic solids this means every admissible nonzero deformation costs positive strain energy. It requires stable material properties, valid elements and enough constraints on every connected part to remove rigid motions. Merely adding one support does not establish these conditions.

A preconditioner cheaply approximates the inverse action of A. Call its matrix M; applying it means solving approximately with M, not explicitly inverting it. Preconditioned CG also requires a fixed, symmetric positive-definite preconditioner. KoFEM uses MFEM’s symmetric Gauss–Seidel: one forward and one backward sweep per application. These local sweeps reduce error cheaply but can remove long-range, slowly varying errors poorly.

Which residual does the solver measure?

At iteration k, the estimate xk has algebraic residual rk:

rk=b−Axk.

On free solid rows this is the remaining nodal force imbalance, in N. Constrained rows enforce prescribed values; their residual is not a support reaction. The scalar in KoFEM’s solid log and continuation dialog uses a preconditioned norm:

ηk=rkTM−1rk,ρk=ηkη0.

η0 is the norm at the start of this solve, before the first CG step. The displayed ρk is dimensionless. It is neither a maximum force imbalance nor the ordinary Euclidean residual divided by the load norm. With stiffness units for M, ηk2 has energy units, N·mm. MFEM updates the residual recursively during CG; the wrapper does not independently recompute the final force imbalance or estimate a condition number.

Tolerances and stopping

The relative tolerance τrel asks for a reduction from the initial norm. The absolute tolerance τabs sets a floor in the same units as ηk. MFEM tests the squared quantities:

ηk2≤max(τrel2η02,τabs2).

Thus either tolerance can allow stopping. A relative tolerance of 10−6 asks for a millionfold norm reduction, or a factor of 10−12 in the squared test. It does not promise six correct digits of displacement or stress.

The app exposes continuation, not an ordinary solid-tolerance control. Optimization tolerance inputs belong to another solver path. With zero absolute tolerance, there is no positive absolute stopping floor. If η0=​0, MFEM accepts the initial iterate immediately; the ratio ηk/η0 is undefined. The progress monitor substitutes zero in that case, while the final MFEM ratio can be non-finite. Treat it as a zero-initial-residual edge case, not a numerical accuracy estimate.

Worked example: two free bar DOFs

Take two axial bar elements of stiffness 1 N/mm each. Fix the left node and apply 1 N to each of the other two nodes. Their free displacements are x1 and x2, in mm. The reduced system is:

A=[2−1−11]

b=​(1,1)T N. Equilibrium gives x=​(2,3)T mm: the first element carries 2 N, the second 1 N. Start CG at zero. For the forward/backward Gauss–Seidel pair, the inverse action is:

M−1=[3/41/21/21]

Initially M−1r0=​(5/4,3/2)T mm and η02=​11/4 N·mm. The first CG step length is 22/13. The resulting iterates are:

Conditioning, scaling and rigid modes

Conditioning describes how strongly a solution responds to perturbations. For a symmetric positive-definite matrix, the spectral condition number κ2(A) is the largest eigenvalue divided by the smallest. Widely separated stiffness scales make this ratio large. CG depends on the spectrum after preconditioning; a good preconditioner clusters those scales.

Residual size alone cannot bound displacement error without information about the inverse stiffness. Let x denote the exact solution of these discrete equations and ek=​x−xk the algebraic error. Then Aek=​rk. For nonzero load and solution, ordinary Euclidean norms satisfy:

|ek|2|x|2≤κ2(A)|rk|2|b|2.

This bound uses different norms from the reported ρk; inserting the log value into it would be unjustified. In practice:

  • Rigid modes: missing supports or disconnected bodies make the matrix singular. Even a zero residual cannot establish uniqueness: two free nodes joined by a spring with balanced loads have the same residual after any common translation. See Rigid-body modes.
  • Near mechanisms and constraints: a weak connection or poorly placed supports leave a very soft motion. Extra constraints can remove it but also change the physical problem and make the answer too stiff. Support placement is a modelling choice.
  • Material contrast: very stiff and very soft regions create separated scales. Poisson’s ratio near 0.5 makes the volumetric stiffness large and can also cause displacement-element locking; a tighter solve does not remove that discretisation error.
  • Mesh quality and size: distorted elements, small features and large size transitions can worsen conditioning. Refining adds unknowns and can increase the iteration count. Correct poor elements first; refinement alone is not a solver repair.
  • Scaling: consistent units and suitable variable scaling keep magnitudes manageable. Coherent unit conversion preserves the physical solution; changing only the modulus or coordinates changes the model. Multiplying a matrix by one scalar does not change its condition number. KoFEM’s solid wrapper adds no separate equilibration step beyond preconditioning.

Iteration budgets and failure

A budget limits work; it is not a convergence criterion. At the initial 5000-step solid budget, KoFEM can pause at Keep solving? when a recent residual window shows progress. The policy checks both window endpoints and the geometric means of its two halves; it tolerates oscillations but requires improvement beyond roundoff. The default window is 100 observations. This heuristic predicts neither eventual convergence nor the validity of the model.

Continue adds the requested number of iterations to the live loop. It preserves the iterate, residual, search direction, preconditioner and original convergence target. The prompt may recur. Stop, including Escape, raises an explicit error and discards the partial result. Without a callback, or when the progress policy rejects continuation, exhausting the budget fails.

The solid wrapper checks GetConverged() before recovering results. Failure reports the relative residual, iteration count and target 1e-6, with “The partial result was discarded”. MFEM can also warn about a non-positive preconditioner or operator during a breakdown. A non-convergence message alone does not diagnose singularity; a constrained but poorly conditioned model can also fail.

Check body connectivity and supports first, then material values and units, element quality and stiffness contrasts. Inspect the displacement pattern and residual history if available. A falling residual may justify more iterations; a plateau calls for investigating conditioning. Although the current error suggests refining the mesh, refinement can make convergence harder.

Judge the displacement or stress quantity needed for the engineering decision. The axial-bar benchmark checks extension against a known solution. A single passing benchmark does not validate all meshes or boundary conditions. The result interpretation chapter develops these checks further.

KoFEM implementation

KoFEM uses MFEM 4.7 for the solid solve.

  • FormLinearSystem → CG → RecoverFEMSolution connects assembly to the solid solve. solve_mfem_cg chooses CG, the smoother and relative tolerance; its monitor reports progress every ten iterations and on completion.
  • MFEM’s GSSmoother defaults and forward/backward application establish the preconditioner. The base constructor initializes absolute tolerance to zero; CGSolver::Mult defines the preconditioned norm and squared stopping test. GetFinalRelNorm returns the final-to-initial norm ratio.
  • CgContinuation owns the progress policy and budgets; browser controls, worker request and continuation dialog connect it to the app. The worker returns errors to the caller rather than returning a partial field.

The worked example above was checked independently of the production solver; it illustrates the equations, not KoFEM’s code path. KoFEM’s automated tests drive the real solid solver through successful continuation, Stop and an exhausted iteration budget, and check that a continued solve gives the same final field as an uninterrupted one.

Why KoFEM uses this method

Choice and rationale. Sparse CG with symmetric Gauss–Seidel suits a constrained, positive-definite elastic system and keeps storage and per-step work modest in the browser. The implementation has a fixed accuracy target, checks convergence and lets a progressing solve continue without restarting.

Tradeoff and alternatives. Local smoothing gives no mesh-independent iteration guarantee. Algebraic multigrid with elasticity near-null-space information, geometric multigrid and stronger smoothing are alternatives, at the cost of extra WebAssembly dependencies or a mesh hierarchy. Sparse direct factorization is another general option, with fill-in and memory costs; the current solid path does not select it as a fallback. These are options, not evidence of a completed comparative benchmark.

Limits. A correctly constrained but very large or poorly conditioned model may still stagnate or need many extensions. Continuation cannot repair a rigid mode or make a wrong physical model accurate.
