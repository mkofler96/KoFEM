How do supported load and displacement conditions enter that system?

Assembly produces a stiffness matrix K and a load vector f. Supports change which equations are solved; loads change only f. Without supports, K is singular: the body can move as a rigid object without straining. This page shows how each condition enters the system and how each can change the answer. Units are N, mm and MPa, as in the consistent-unit reference. Shells and solid–shell couplings also carry rotations; Shell formulation and Solid–shell coupling cover them.

Two kinds of boundary condition

The weak form of static equilibrium reads:

∫Ωε(w):σdV=∫Ωw·bdV+∫ΓNw·tdS.

The two parts of the boundary enter in different ways:

  • Essential (displacement) conditions on ΓD set the displacement itself: u=​uD, where uD is the prescribed value in mm. They act on the unknowns directly, and the test displacement w is zero there. A fixed support is the special case uD=​0.
  • Natural (load) conditions on ΓN prescribe a traction t in MPa. They appear only as work on the right-hand side of the weak form. A free surface with no load is also a natural condition, t=​0; it needs no input.

The body force b (N/mm³) acts in the volume. A condition can be essential in one component and natural in the others: a roller fixes the normal displacement and leaves the tangential traction at zero.

Supports: fixed, per component and prescribed

On a solid, each node has three translational DOFs, ux, uy and uz, shown in the app as Ux, Uy and Uz. A support constrains some of them:

Support types on a solid node
SupportConstrainedTypical use
FixedUx = Uy = Uz = 0A welded or clamped face
Per componentOnly the chosen components = 0A symmetry plane or sliding support (roller); a minimal 3-2-1 support
PrescribedChosen components = a non-zero value in mmA known imposed motion, such as a press stroke or a fit-up offset

In the app, a boundary-condition group combines selected faces or points, the components to constrain and one value in mm. A value of 0 fixes those components; any other value prescribes it for each checked component. Solid nodes have no rotational DOFs, so Rx, Ry and Rz appear only when the model contains shells or a coupling reference point. Constraining one solid node cannot stop a rotation; constrain several nodes, or use a coupling.

A prescribed displacement can drive the model on its own: a model with prescribed values and no load is valid. The app enables Solve once the model has at least one support and either a load or a non-zero prescribed value.

How constraints change the system

Split the DOFs into free ones (F) and constrained ones (C). The assembled system Kd=​f+r contains unknown reactions r, nonzero only at constrained DOFs. Written in blocks, with dC known:

KFFdF=fF−KFCdC,rC=KCFdF+KCCdC−fC.

The solver solves the first equation. A prescribed value moves to the right-hand side as an equivalent load, −KFCdC. For a fixed support, dC=​0 and that term vanishes. The second equation recovers the support reactions afterwards.

MFEM, the finite element library KoFEM uses, keeps the full matrix instead of deleting rows. It zeroes the off-diagonal entries in each constrained row and column and keeps the diagonal. Each constrained row then reads Kiidi=​Kiigi, where gi is the prescribed value of DOF i, so di=​gi. The free rows are exactly the first equation above. Two consequences follow:

  • A load on a constrained DOF has no effect on the displacement. It goes straight into the support. A force on a fully fixed face, for example, does nothing.
  • Reactions are not part of the solve. KoFEM does not report them at the reviewed revision; check support forces with a free-body diagram.

Worked example: a prescribed displacement

Reuse the two-element bar from Assembly: L=​1000 mm, A=​10000 mm2, E=​210000 MPa, element stiffness k=​4.2×106 N/mm. Fix node 1 (d1=​0), pull node 3 to d3=​0.1 mm and apply no force. Node 2 is the only free DOF.

K=k[1−10−12−10−11]

Row 2 gives 2kd2=​0−(−k)(0)−(−k)(0.1 mm), so the prescribed value acts as a load of 0.1k=​4.2×105 N on node 2. After MFEM’s elimination:

Kelim=k[100020001]

The right-hand side becomes (k×0,0.1k,k×0.1): rows 1 and 3 restate the prescribed values, row 2 carries their equivalent load. Solving gives d2=​0.1k/(2k)=​0.05 mm, so d=​(0,0.05,0.1) mm.

The reactions are r1=​k(d1−d2)=​−2.1×105 N and r3=​k(d3−d2)=​2.1×105 N. They balance. As a check, a bar stretched by δ needs EAδ/L=​210000×10000×0.1/1000 N=​2.1×105 N. The axial stress is Eδ/L=​21 MPa. In KoFEM, the 3D bar with a sliding root face and Ux = 0.1 mm prescribed on its far face gives element von Mises stresses within 0.2% of 21 MPa; the small spread comes from the solver tolerance.

Loads: force, traction, pressure and body force

Every load becomes work-equivalent nodal forces in f, in N. The type of input decides how the load is distributed:

Load types on a solid at the reviewed revision
LoadInputInto f
Force on facesTotal vector F, NUniform traction t=​F/A over the group’s whole area A, integrated as ∫NatdS
Pressure on facesScalar pload, MPaTraction t=​−ploadn: positive pushes into the surface; follows curved faces
Force on pointsTotal vector, NSplit equally over the picked nodes and added directly
Moment on facesVector M, N·mmTangential nodal forces about each selected face’s centroid, zero net force
Body force—Not applied: the static solid solve has no gravity or other volume load

A force is a total, not a force per area: adding a second face to a force group spreads the same total over a larger area. A pressure is per area: a larger selection gives a larger resultant. Loads superpose: two groups on the same face both act, and their effects add. The static solve does not use the stored material density.

A moment group works differently: it applies the full moment on each selected face. Two face selections in one moment group apply twice the moment. To apply it once, select one face, or divide the moment by the number of faces.

Worked example: from total force to traction

Pull the 100 mm × 100 mm end face of the bar with F=​106 N in x. The face area is A=​104 mm2, so:

tx=FA=106 N104 mm2=100 N/mm2=100 MPa.

The engine logs the same conversion for this case: force → traction [100 0 0] … (A=10000). Now mesh the face with 2 × 2 bilinear quadrilaterals of 50 mm × 50 mm. Each corner function of a quadrilateral integrates to a quarter of its area, ∫NadS=​2500/4 mm2=​625 mm2, so every element gives 100×625 N=​62500 N to each of its four nodes. A node collects one share per adjacent element:

Nodal forces on a 2 × 2 face, F=​106 N
NodeCountElementsForce eachSubtotal
Corner4162 500 N250 000 N
Edge midpoint42125 000 N500 000 N
Centre14250 000 N250 000 N
Total91 000 000 N

The resultant is F again, and by symmetry it acts at the face centre. Splitting F equally, 106/9 N≈​111111 N per node, gives the same total but the wrong distribution. On an uneven mesh it also moves the resultant. Split the same face into two strips, 20 mm and 80 mm wide, with nodes at y=​0,20,100 mm. Work-equivalent shares put the resultant at y=​50 mm, the face centroid. An equal split puts it at y=​(0+20+100)/3 mm=​40 mm: a spurious moment of 106 N×10 mm=​107 N·mm.

A pressure of pload=​1 MPa on the same face, whose outward normal is +x, gives t=​(−1,0,0) MPa and a resultant of −104 N in x: it pushes the face in. On a linear triangle, each corner receives one third of the face force; the quadratic shares are on Elements and quadrature.

Rigid-body modes

A rigid motion moves a body without straining it. For small displacements, every rigid motion is a translation plus a small rotation:

u(x)=a+θ×x.

The translation a (mm) and rotation θ (rad) have three components each: six rigid-body modes in 3D. The strain of every such field is zero, so Kdrigid=​0. In the bar, d=​(1,1,1) is a rigid translation, and each row of K sums to zero. A rigid mode costs no energy, so nothing in K resists it. Only constraints can.

Every body that is not connected to another must be held against all six modes. A 3-2-1 support does exactly this with six components at three points: A fixes Ux, Uy and Uz; B, along x from A, fixes Uy and Uz; C, offset from that line in y, fixes Uz.

For a balanced load, such as equal and opposite forces on two faces, a 3-2-1 support carries no reaction and does not disturb the stress. In KoFEM, the 1000 mm bar pulled by ±106 N on its end faces and held this way stretches by 0.476190 mm, matching PL/(EA)=​0.476190 mm, with 100.0 MPa in every element.

Too few constraints

If any rigid mode remains, KFF is singular and the equations have no unique solution. KoFEM then either stops with an error, or returns a result that contains an arbitrary rigid motion. The second outcome is more dangerous, because it looks like a success. Both occur on the bar with only Ux fixed on its root face, which leaves Uy, Uz and the rotation about x free:

  • The load excites a free mode. A transverse force of 1000 N in y has nothing to react against. The iterative solver cannot converge, and the solve stops with “CG solver did not converge” after 5000 iterations.
  • The load is orthogonal to every free mode. The axial force does no work in the free modes, so the solver converges. Elongation and stress are correct, but the displacement contains an arbitrary rigid part. Here the largest Uy was 0.0097 mm, against 0.0143 mm with a 3-2-1 support; that difference is a rigid shift, not deformation.

KoFEM requires at least one support before it solves, but it does not check that the supports remove all six modes. Common causes of a free mode are:

  • a single roller or symmetry plane with nothing holding the in-plane directions;
  • point supports on one straight line, which leave the rotation about that line free; and
  • an assembly whose bodies touch but share no nodes and are not joined by a tie: each loose body has its own six modes.

Too many constraints

Constraining a motion that the real support allows makes the model too stiff: each extra constraint can only add stiffness. Constraining more than six DOFs is normal in itself; a fixed face constrains hundreds.

Fixed instead of sliding. A fully fixed face stops the Poisson contraction that a real end plate might allow. Pull a 100 mm long bar of the same section with 106 N. With a sliding root face (Ux only, plus a 3-2-1 point set), KoFEM reproduces PL/(EA)=​0.04762 mm and a uniform 100 MPa. With the face fully fixed, the mean extension is about 3% smaller (0.0457, 0.0460 and 0.0461 mm on 4³, 8³ and 16³ hexahedral meshes). The peak von Mises stress at the edge of the fixed face rises from 102.7 to 127.3 MPa over the same refinements. That peak is a singularity: it grows with refinement and has no converged value. For the 1000 mm bar, the stiffening is 0.5%, because the effect stays near the support.

Point supports that carry load. A constraint at one node concentrates its reaction there. Hold the 1000 mm bar by the 3-2-1 points alone and pull only one end: corner A now reacts the whole 106 N. The solve succeeds, but the element at A reaches 1741 MPa von Mises, and the off-centre reaction bends the bar: mid-span stresses range from 32 to 294 MPa instead of a uniform 100 MPa. Use point supports to remove rigid modes, not to carry load.

Prescribing more components than intended. A prescribed value applies to every checked component. To pull a face in x without also holding it in y and z, check only Ux.

Conflicting constraints and unmatched selections

Several groups can name the same DOF, for example at the nodes on the edge shared by two selected faces. At the reviewed revision, the solid solve handles overlaps and stale selections as follows:

  • Same DOF, same value in two groups: accepted and merged into one constraint.
  • Same DOF, two different values (fixed by one group and prescribed by another, or prescribed twice): the solve stops with an error naming the vertex and component.
  • Two loads on the same face: the solve integrates both and adds them.
  • Some selected faces are not on the boundary: the log warns with their count, and they add no load. A force total is spread over the matched area; a pressure loses that area.
  • No selected face is on the boundary: the solve stops with an error.
  • Order-2 elements: an edge’s midside DOF is constrained when both of its end nodes are, to their average value. Edges leaving a support stay free.

To resolve a conflict, remove the shared nodes from one group, or give both groups the same value. On order-2 hexahedra, which the app’s mesher does not produce, the centre node of a supported face is not constrained.

What to check when a result looks wrong

Start from the symptom.

Singular: no convergence, or huge or arbitrary displacements.

  • For each separate body, list which of the three translations and three rotations its supports stop. The supports must stop all six.
  • Check per-component supports: a roller stops only one direction.
  • Check that point supports are not on one line.
  • Check that touching bodies share nodes or are joined by a tie.
  • Compare the displacement pattern with a rigid motion. A body that translates or turns without straining has a free mode.

Unexpectedly stiff: too little displacement.

  • Ask whether each fixed face is really fixed in all three directions, or should slide. Try a sliding support and compare.
  • Check that no load sits on a constrained face or node, where it has no effect.
  • Check force versus pressure: a force is a total in N, a pressure is per area in MPa. 1 MPa on 100 mm² is 100 N.
  • Check prescribed groups for extra components.
  • Check units: a modulus entered in Pa with lengths in mm is a million times too stiff. See Consistent unit systems.
  • Thin walls of linear tetrahedra lock in bending; see Elements and quadrature.

Peak stresses at the edge of a support or under a point load are usually singular. Judge the result away from them. Linear solve explains how the solver reports failure.

KoFEM implementation and evidence

Reviewed on 2 November 2026 at 41aa05f, against MFEM 4.7. The solid solve is solve_linear_elastic.

  • Groups to payload. Each node of a support group gets the group’s value per checked component (boundaryRebuild.ts). The solid path sorts zero values into fully fixed vertices or single components and non-zero values into prescribed DOFs, ignoring rotations (solveMeshPacking.ts). Solve requires a support and a load or a non-zero prescribed value (useSolver.ts).
  • Constraint collection. Fixed, per-component and prescribed DOFs are collected, extended to order-2 edge DOFs, and checked for conflicts (set_dirichlet, edge extension, conflict check).
  • Elimination. Prescribed values are written into the solution vector before FormLinearSystem; MFEM keeps the diagonal (default policy) and moves the known values to the right-hand side (EliminateVDofsInRHS).
  • Surface loads. One surface load per force or pressure group (rebuildSurfaceLoads); the engine matches faces to boundary elements, warns or stops on unmatched faces, divides a force by the matched area and integrates the traction (apply_surface_loads, traction coefficient).
  • Point loads and moments. Point picks share the group total; moments become nodal forces per face (rebuildLoads, momentLoad.ts); nodal forces are added to f after assembly (apply_point_loads).
