Reading a study

How do geometry, materials, constraints, loads, the FEM discretisation, and results form one study?

Follow a bar pulled along its length. Each modelling choice becomes an input to the equations; each numerical result needs a defined comparison. The axial-bar verification provides the runnable model, measured values and source references for this path.

Start with a question

How far does the loaded end extend, and does the stress in the middle match uniform tension? These are two different quantities. Define them before viewing a contour: mean axial displacement on the end face, and unsmoothed element-centre von Mises stress in a fixed middle region.

Choose the physical model

Use a straight, 1000 mm long bar with a 100 mm square section. Treat it as a static, small-strain, homogeneous isotropic elastic solid, with Young’s modulus 210000 MPa and Poisson’s ratio 0.3. Fix all translations on one end and pull the other with a total axial force of 1000000 N. The remaining faces are traction-free. Ignore gravity, inertia, plasticity and thermal effects.

The numbers use N, mm and MPa. The model introduction explains assumptions; the unit reference explains how coherent systems differ. KoFEM does not convert an inconsistent set of inputs.

Connect displacement to equilibrium

Displacement u gives small strain ε through the symmetric gradient. The isotropic law gives stress σ:

ε=12(∇u+(∇u)T),σ=λtr(ε)I+2με.

The Lamé constants λ,μ follow from the modulus and Poisson’s ratio; I is the identity tensor. Strain is dimensionless and stress is in MPa. Gradient and divergence explains the derivatives; tensor products explains the trace and contractions.

Static equilibrium requires ∇·σ=​0 here, since there is no body force. A fixed end prescribes displacement; the loaded end prescribes traction. Multiplying equilibrium by a test displacement and integrating by parts gives the weak form: internal virtual work equals applied virtual work. The full linear-elasticity and equilibrium chapters are still planned; this linked derivation supplies the weak form used in this study.

Build and solve a discrete study

Split the volume into hexahedra and approximate displacement with continuous shape functions. The reference mapping transfers derivatives and integrals to each physical element. Elements and quadrature explains the approximation; assembly adds element stiffnesses into the global matrix.

The load and constraint rules integrate a uniform end traction into work-equivalent nodal forces and eliminate prescribed translations. The linear solver then computes displacement coefficients. Its residual tests the discrete equations; it does not measure the physical prediction error.

Check the answer

For uniform one-dimensional tension, the extension is δ=​PL/(EA) and axial stress is σxx=​P/A, where P is total force, L length, E Young’s modulus and A section area. These give approximately 0.476190 mm and 100 MPa. The fully fixed 3D end suppresses Poisson contraction, so its extension need not equal the one-dimensional answer exactly.

Displacement and stress distinguishes solver samples from displayed averages. Interpretation and convergence explains what to compare. The measured refinement table checks both quantities and explains the remaining discrepancy.
